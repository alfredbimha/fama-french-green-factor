Understanding Factor Models in Finance

How the Fama-French model explains stock returns — and whether a 'green factor' adds explanatory power.

The Big Idea: Risk Factors

Why do some stocks earn higher returns than others? Finance theory says: because they carry more risk. The Fama-French model identifies specific risks that investors are compensated for.

Market (Mkt-RF)

Stocks go up and down with the overall market. This is the most basic risk.

Size (SMB)

"Small Minus Big" — Small companies are riskier but earn higher returns on average.

Value (HML)

"High Minus Low" — Cheap (value) stocks earn more than expensive (growth) stocks.

Our Innovation: The Green Factor (GMB)

We construct a Green-Minus-Brown factor:

GMB = Average return of clean energy ETFs − Average return of fossil fuel ETFs

If GMB is a real risk factor, it should explain returns that the traditional FF3 model cannot. When we add GMB to the model:

R − Rf = α + β₁(Mkt-RF) + β₂(SMB) + β₃(HML) + β₄(GMB) + ε

🌿 Green ETFs

Positive GMB beta (~0.4) — they move with the green factor.

β = +0.38 to +0.47

🛢️ Brown ETFs

Negative GMB beta (~−0.5) — they move opposite to the green factor.

β = −0.48 to −0.75
Key finding: GMB is highly significant (p < 0.0001) for all ETFs, and has low correlation with traditional FF3 factors. This suggests "green risk" is a distinct, priced factor in equity markets.
